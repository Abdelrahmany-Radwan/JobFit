For the curious

No magic box. Here’s what’s actually happening.

01ParsePDF, DOCX, or text is read locally.
→
02RepresentMiniLM turns sentences into embeddings.
→
03CompareCosine similarity and lexical signals rank evidence.
→
04ExplainResults link back to the original resume text.

Why embeddings? “Built dashboards from operational data” can support a role asking for “data visualization” even when the exact phrase is missing. Semantic similarity helps recognize that relationship.

Why a fallback? If the browser cannot load the embedding model, JobFit keeps working with lexical similarity instead of turning a model-loading problem into a broken product.

Questions people actually ask

Quick answers before you trust it with your résumé.

Does JobFit store my résumé?

No. The public app parses your résumé in the browser and does not require a JobFit application backend to store the document.

Is the percentage an ATS or hiring score?

No. It is an evidence-alignment signal inside JobFit. It helps rank how strongly existing résumé text supports the requirements it finds.

Does JobFit rewrite or invent experience?

No. The product is deliberately evidence-first: matches point back to text already present in the résumé.

What files can I use?

PDF, DOCX, TXT, or pasted text. PDF.js and Mammoth.js handle document extraction client-side.

What happens if the embedding model does not load?

JobFit falls back to lexical similarity instead of failing the entire experience.

Why MiniLM?

MiniLM gives JobFit compact sentence embeddings that are practical enough to run in-browser while still capturing semantic similarity beyond exact keywords.
