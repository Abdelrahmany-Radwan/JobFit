The problem I wanted to solve

I kept seeing the same problem.

Students have real projects, classes, internships, and leadership experience—but translating that experience to one specific job description is difficult.

Keyword scanners can miss related experience when the wording is different. Generative tools can go too far in the other direction and rewrite a resume into something the student cannot confidently defend.

So I built JobFit around one rule: use technology to understand the match, but keep the evidence grounded in what the student actually did.

Simple enough to explain. Technical enough to matter.

03CompareCosine similarity and lexical signals rank evidence.

04ExplainResults link back to the original resume text.

Why embeddings? “Built dashboards from operational data” can support a role asking for “data visualization” even when the exact phrase is missing. Semantic similarity helps recognize that relationship.

Why a fallback? If the browser cannot load the embedding model, JobFit keeps working with lexical similarity instead of turning a model-loading problem into a broken product.
